<p>10: Will<br/>As expected, the Bengal bye week bungled this team. Even so, it's not like Will is decent anyway. Since week 2, he's averaged 72 points per week, and is now dead last in PF and standings. I don't see this team losing out or anything. With Green and Mixon returning, and Ajayi finally turning in a good performance (the Dolphins should begin leaning on him more, since it clearly leads to wins), there are going to be some 90+ point weeks that earn dubs. We have to face reality, though. Will is already at 5 losses, and a single additional one ends his season. He's just playing for pride now.<br/><br/>9: Ashwin<br/>All season I've harped about how volatile this team is, making it tough to judge. Well, it's becoming clear now that the good days were flukes. Two weeks above 109 are countered by four weeks below 78, and last week's 51.4 stands as the low point of the season so far. The strength of this team was supposed to lie in its formidable WR corps, yet it currently doesn't have a WR ranked above 25. The Falcons offense is sputtering and taking Julio down with it, Landry and Baldwin are inconsistent, and the Saints are no longer in offensive shootouts every week to help Thomas's production. There are basically no RBs on this team except for a highly unpredictable Montgomery. Rodgers's injury (boost for Ty) plus the return of Dak should help out, but Ashwin is going nowhere fast.<br/><br/>8: Anish<br/>Scoring 116.6 without Ezekiel Elliott is truly a remarkable feat. Just Anish's luck that it came against the highest score of the season. What went right? Cousins had a stellar day - somewhat unsustainable, but a good sign for the future. Demarco scored his second touchdown of the season - he was still outcarried and outperformed by Henry, so this is iffy. AB absolutely went off, and the Steelers won a big upset - expect more of that, then. Tate had a good day - he's very inconsistent. And the Vikings D shut down the Rodgers-less Packers. Is all this sustainable? Well, no. But Zeke is returning, and barring another series of suspensions and unsuspensions, maybe Anish can eke out a few more good weeks. Don't count on a surprise playoff push or anything, though.<br/><br/>7: Sid<br/>Things were almost hopeless after the 0-4 start, but Sid hung in there and is now 2-4. It's not ideal, but this isn't an impossible situation. 8-5 is always the benchmark for playoff contention (though sometimes a high PF is also needed), so NoBell's goal is to finish the season 6-1. This has been done before (Adit in 2014), but it's going to require that Sid scores more than 87 every week. In fact, he's probably going to need more than 100 every week, especially in the last three weeks. Most of that rests in the hands of Brees and Bell. Next week is a huge matchup against Brian. Sid cannot afford to lose this.<br/><br/>6: Arjun<br/>It's been a depressing two weeks for this team and fanbase. After a historically bad week 5, Arjun rebounded in week 6, but still couldn't win against an Absolute 0 team without its quarterback, Aaron Rodgers. It appears that scoring around 90 is going to keep being a theme for Arjun; he's scored within 4 points of 90 four out of the six weeks. The good news is that outside of a few tough games (like this coming week), his schedule is mostly reasonable. There is a huge amount of talent on this team, so don't forget about it. A huge key will be how Nelson can perform without his star QB at the helm. <br/><br/>5: Girish<br/>A four-game win streak has to command respect, no matter how it was achieved. Hitting six will land Girish in the record books for 3rd place. In his way stand Ruda and Sid, who will be tough tests for a team that doesn't consistently score high. Girish's entire schedule stays fairly tough until the last two weeks - theoretically easy wins against Will and Anish. Girish's goal at this point should be 9-4, which means playoff lock. If he can win those last two games, he just needs to be 7-4 at week 11. That means going 3-2 in the next five weeks - a very doable task. Girish just has to step up his scoring a little. A shift from 90ish to 100ish a week can make all the difference. Can AP be the key?<br/><br/>4: Akash<br/>Oh, what could have been. This really, truly was the best team of the season. Then DJ got hurt. Then Akash faced a ridiculously difficult schedule. And now, out goes Rodgers for the season. Akash still managed to win and bring himself to 3-3, though. If he can find a serviceable replacement for Rodgers, this is still a playoff-caliber team. He's probably aiming for 8-5, so he'll have to finish 5-2... no easy task in this tough division. It's also worth nothing that Akash got 41 points from his D/ST and K last week, which is obviously unsustainable. And there's also a near-guaranteed week 8 loss to Brian thanks to byes. A playoff push is possible, but will take some miracles. This may end up being one of the sadder stories of the season.<br/><br/>3: Ruda<br/>Hotel Beckham did a solid job without its franchise player, but it wasn't enough to beat Brian. Over-dependence on the Chiefs is becoming painfully obvious, and Ruda knows it. If he can trade one of them away for good return, he'll set himself up for a good second half of the season. An X-factor could be Mark Ingram, who seems to have benefitted greatly from the AP trade (though you never know with Sean Payton). Ruda is 3-3 (though he leads the league in PF), and basically in the same boat as Akash, but has some winnable games in the next few weeks and a better team, thanks to Akash's injuries. That 5-2 finish is actually pretty possible.<br/><br/>2: Brian<br/>Questions about Brian's recent scoring drop-off were answered viciously. Dezpacito roared back to life with 114 points and took down Ruda, leaving Brian at 5-1, already tying his previous high for season wins. What makes this team scary is that it doesn't appear to be over-dependent on New England like Ruda. Other playmakers like Martin, Thompson, and Fuller contribute just as much to this team as the Patriots, and the depth is still fantastic. We can't ignore some of the lower scores in weeks 4 and 5, so Brian has to be able to win against teams like Sid this next week to prove he's worthy of contention for first seed. Still, there's little to complain about here. This team will be around come December.<br/><br/>1: Adit<br/>131.6 is the highest score of any team on the season. Adit has averaged an unbelievable 122 points over the past three weeks. Sure, that's unsustainable and a slight fall back to earth is inevitable. But there's no denying it: the Brittish Empire is the best team in the league right now. Cam and Wentz are both top 5 QBs. Gordon and Howard are absolutely killing it. Crabtree and Adams are both top 10 WRs. Ertz is playing at a level of consistency nearly unseen at the tight end position. And McKinnon, a disappointment for Adit last year, has exploded into one of the best waiver pickups of the year. Depth is a big question mark, mainly due to repeated and enduring injuries. The coming bye weeks could put a damper on Adit's hot streak. Still, this is a 5-1 team that isn't just eyeing the playoffs. First seed and even 10 wins are the goals right now. His schedule is no cake walk, but that hasn't stopped him before. Adit owns this league, for now.<br/></p>
