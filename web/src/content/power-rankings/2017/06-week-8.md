<p>10: Will<br/>The season is officially over for Will, after just seven games. 6 losses eliminates him from playoff contention, and he'll spend the remainder of the season trying to avoid the wrong end of the record books. To do that, he'll need to go at least 2-4 and average just a few more points per game. Both are doable, so I expect his playing-for-pride finish to be successful. <br/><br/>9: Ashwin<br/>A once-promising season has turned into a complete dud for Ashwin. The Pani Puris actually hit 100 for the third time this season, but were still blown out by a freakish performance from Anish. Like Anish, Ashwin will have to run the table to have a playoff shot. Is it possible? Probably not. This RB corps is the worst in the league now that Aaron Jones has usurped the starting RB job from Ty Montgomery. The WR corps is strong as ever, and ASJ has been a huge pickup at TE, so Ashwin should pull out a few wins here and there. But the bottom line in fantasy is to rock the running back position, and he basically has none. <br/><br/>8: Anish<br/>What just happened? No seriously, what? This is perhaps the most confusing and volatile team of all time. 140.5 drew Anish close to the record books (3rd: 147 by Akash) and absolutely shattered the league's 2017 high of 131.6. And this was after putting up 116 last week. It's a testament to how bad this team was before that that Anish still ranks only 6th in PF. Is the recent success sustainable? Cousins and AB should continue to perform at high levels, and though Zeke won't approach 40 weekly, he will provide chunks of points. The two Murrays are hard to gauge; both have major split carry problems, but both have had huge games in the past two weeks. If they can keep producing, Anish should flirt with 100 more often than not, even with Reed's inevitable regression. Running the table won't be easy, but don't write him off yet. First, he has a scary matchup with Ruda.<br/><br/>7: Sid<br/>And here's exactly why you shouldn't write teams off. Sid has turned an 0-4 start into a three week win streak, averaging 104 points per game in that time. Interestingly, Drew Brees has not been a huge factor in this success. The emergence of Aaron Jones and Evan "Travis Kelce" Engram have provided this team with solid production from all positions. If Amari Cooper has truly returned, perhaps Sid can continue to win against good teams. This won't be easy, though, with a tough schedule ahead. Major byes this week could stall the winning streak.<br/><br/>6: Girish<br/>The string of wins finally came to an end in week 7, but in the closest game of the season. Girish lost by 0.4; even one catch by Eric Decker would have made the difference. At 4-3, the path to the playoffs is clear but difficult. Sid, Brian, and Arjun await this team in the coming weeks, and may put a damper in their attempt to finish 4-2 to put themselves in playoff contention. Girish has proven he can consistently score in the 90's, but he will need to take it a step further to make the postseason. The biggest key to that will be McCoy; has McDermott finally learned to trust his star RB in the redzone? Also important will be McCaffrey; he's had a few weeks of 9-12 points, which will serve Girish perfectly from the flex position if he can keep it up. Let's see if the Fantastic Gore can handle the upcoming schedule. <br/><br/>5: Arjun<br/>An ugly win is still a win, and it has kept Arjun right in the thick of the running for the West. There is no getting around the fact that this team is not the best at scoring, however. It cannot seem to crack 100, and increasingly dips into slump weeks. What needs to change? Well, Alex Smith is doing a fine job replacing Matt Ryan and should keep it up. Devonta Freeman and Carlos Hyde is actually an underrated and fearsome RB duo, and will no doubt rebound from last week, when they combined for 17.8 points. Trading away Matt Bryant and further decreasing reliance on Atlanta was a smart move. All of these are strengths for Arjun. The weakness? Wide receiver. Nelson and Fitzgerald sounded like a top-end WR duo two weeks ago, but now both their starting QBs have been hurt badly and it showed, as they combined for just 4.2 points in week 7. Arjun needs an answer at this position (Desean Jackson, perhaps?) if he's to compete for the West. <br/><br/>4: Akash<br/>People have joked that this team is cursed, but it really seems true. Palmer went down for the season in week 7, marking yet another devastating injury for this team. It pulled off a win thanks to a weak opponent, but only scored 83.5 points (probably would have been around 96 if Palmer had stayed healthy). To be fair, Fournette was out. When everyone is playing, Gurley and Fournette can carry this team. They are the #1 and #3 running backs, respectively, which is absolutely insane. In any given week, they can combine for 45+ alone. Problem is, Akash has a huge amount of byes this coming week in a tough matchup against Brian. If he loses that, he'll be sitting at 4-4, and require a 4-1 finish for playoff contention. Akash needs to find a reliable, healthy QB fast. One other point of concern is Akash's reliance on K and D/ST; they have combined for 30 and 41 in the past two weeks. That won't continue, and those points have to come from somewhere. <br/><br/>3: Ruda<br/>Ruda is the new Arjun, but with a harder schedule. 4 straight weeks of scoring in the 90's have kept him afloat, but not stellar, going 2-2 in that time. He squeaked by in week 7 without Deshaun Watson, which was a bigger win than many people give credit for. Had Ruda lost, he would be 3-4 and need a ridiculous 5-1 finish for playoff contention. 4-2 is doable, especially if Ingram continues to dominate the Saints backfield (I guess Payton decided not to hate him anymore). Like Girish, though, Ruda needs to find a way to start hitting the 100's, which may be necessary to beat suddenly hot teams like Anish and Sid. On good weeks for KC, that should be no problem, but when they struggle? It's no wonder some of Ruda's Chiefs are on the trading block. How he handles this situation will be crucial to the remainder of the season. <br/><br/>2: Adit<br/>Week 4: 8 touchdowns. Week 5: 7 touchdowns. Week 6: 10 touchdowns. Week 7: 2 touchdowns. The Brittish Empire is defined by the endzone. Consistent scorers like Crabtree and Gordon skyrocket Adit's touchdown total most weeks, but there are going to be some absolute flops, like this past one. And when that happens, there aren't really any yardage gainers to make up for it. Touchdowns by nature are less consistent than yards. Adit still came within a hair of winning, but the loss puts him at 5-2 and down to second seed for the first time in a while. Looking ahead, the schedule isn't easy, especially thanks to a Week 9 full of byes. To get first seed, he'll have to go 5-1 or 4-2, which should be possible if the touchdowns return. There's a lot of promise in this team's Eagles, and if Philly continues its hot streak and Adit starts playing guys like Wentz and Agholor, he could be looking to snatch that #1 spot right back. <br/><br/>1: Brian<br/>Being first this late is a first for this franchise, but it's well deserved. Brian may have lost this week, but he passed Adit in PF to earn first seed, and seems able to score well more consistently than Adit. This is a team that won't really explode past 100 too far, but can hit right around it week in and week out. It's encouraging that, once again, Brian pulled off a good score despite mediocre performances from his Patriots. Chris Thompson seriously looks like the real deal as an RB1. The Rams D and kicker won't be this good every week (32 combined points), but the return of Fuller will help make up for some loss. This week, Brian gets a theoretical joke of a matchup with an Absolute Zero team wrecked with byes. Getting to 6-2 will be fantastic, and set Brian up for a potential 10-3 or so finish. A trade has even helped take care of Week 9 byes, meaning there isn't a terrible week to dread looking forward. Watch out for this team. </p>
