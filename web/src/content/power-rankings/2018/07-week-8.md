<p>By far the most dramatic week in recent memory, Week 7 featured all five games coming down to Monday night - with some being almost unbelievably close. That’s... all I want to say on the matter. The interesting thing is that after weeks of disappointment, the East completely exploded. This season sure is keeping us on our toes.<br/><br/>10: Ashwin {2-5} (-1)<br/>Nothing was done to defend Ashwin’s rise to 9 last week, and as a result he drops back to a familiar 10th. His 81.2 was the lowest in the league, and he’s averaging just 87 per week. This might be the worst RB situation in the league, and that’s saying something. Antonio Brown can’t do everything, and he won’t start to anytime soon. Good luck winning out bud.<br/><br/>9: Sid {2-5} (-1)<br/>Good news: there are three 2-5 teams<br/><br/>Bad news: Sid is one of them<br/><br/>Good news: Cam and Jeffery are ballers<br/><br/>Bad news: They scored 41% of Sid’s points<br/><br/>Good news: Sid barely lost to an underrated team<br/><br/>Bad news: Sid has to play three teams with current winning records in the next three weeks<br/><br/>Good news: uh<br/><br/>Bad news: Wikipedia describes biting into a Carolina Reaper as “having a fruity taste, with the initial bite being sweet and then immediately turning into ‘molten lava’”<br/><br/>8: Girish {2-5} (+1)<br/>If I had to pick a 2-5 team to pull off a miracle, it’d be this one. Fresh off a 105-pointer, Girish has made a series of intriguing moves to salvage his season. Trading away Julio is always questionable, but considering the Atlanta bye and the need for Girish to essentially win out, perhaps it made sense. Chubb fills a huge hole left by the injury to Lynch, and John Brown has honestly performed ridiculously well this season. Now I wouldn’t look too much into last week’s good performance, but on paper this team looks the most poised of the lower tier to change things up.</p>

<p>7: Arjun {3-4} (-)<br/>Well, you hate to see a decent win not get rewarded with a rankings rise, but unfortunately the teams just ahead of Arjun all did even better than him. This underrated team still leads the division in PF, and stats-wise is really dang good. Arjun has players at the following ranks within their positions: 2, 5, 8, 1, 21, 7, 3, 4, 15. Not bad at all... after a much-needed win, he’ll look to get things rolling.<br/><br/>6: Akash {4-3} (-)<br/>Akash becomes the second team to upset Ruda in a massive shootout, scoring a whopping 128.3 points. What he have yet to see, though, is any semblance of consistency. Aside from three impressive weeks of 109, 132, and 128, Akash has scored 79, 75, 82, and 71. That inspires little confidence, and it’s why we can’t quite raise him yet. Show us something again next week, and Akash can break the top half for the first time all season.<br/><br/>5: Adit {4-3} (-2)<br/>Midway is exactly where this team should be ranked after perhaps the most heart-wrenching loss in league history. The situation is confusing, to say the least. While there are clear superstars, namely Mahomes and Green, a lot of players are very up-in-the-air after trades and injuries. Hyde and Breida have no clear future, and Ito Smith (sniff) showed he’s not a consistent bet right now. What’s interesting was the Warriors’ immediate reaction to trade away Evans for Ingram and Cooper. We don’t know what kind of season Ingram will have yet, but it’s possible he’s the answer this team desperately needs at the position. Or not.</p>

<p>4: Will {4-3} (+1)<br/>Now we’re talking. It seemed like everyone was waiting for this talented team to spring back into action, and it did just that with an exciting victory that brought it back above .500. Will and Adit are both averaging around 100 a week, though recent trends and on-paper talent warrant the former leapfrogging the latter for now. Barkley is unreal, and Will seems to have found his RB2 in Adrian Peterson. Also, Adam Thielen has yet to score less than double digits this year.<br/><br/>3: Anish {4-3} (+1)<br/>The theme this week was “resounding comeback by the East” and spearheading that movement is Anish. Underrated and dangerous, this team quietly slipped into first place in the division and the second seed overall. Anish did exactly what you hope to do with the #1 pick: let him carry you every single week, and surround him with solid players who rarely fall flat. There are no superstars other than Gurley (there don’t need to be any), but there’s a lot of decent depth. Todd gets you 30 a week, the rest combine for 70, and you’re good to go. Watch out for this emotionless blob of Jell-O.</p>

<p>2: Brian {5-2} (-)<br/>Rggrhrreggehhrggeggeg<br/><br/>Rrrrrrfgrrrffrrfrrggrrghr<br/><br/>Excuse me. Brian survived one of the most intense games in league history to move to 5-2, just as top competitor Ruda fell. He’s now in an all-out war for the West, though there’s no doubt he’s a little outmatched for now. Brian continues to sabotage himself with horrible trades; if he had James White and OBJ on his team right now he’d have a great case to be first. Still, the alternative isn’t awful, especially considering he proved he isn’t completely dependent on Melvin Gordon. Trubisky’s performance (for a few weeks now) does seem flukey, but with Rodgers casually chilling on the bench, Brian is in good shape. I hope he dies.<br/><br/>1: Ruda {5-2} (-)<br/>It takes an absolute shootout to take down Ruda, and that’s exactly what Akash did. There isn’t much to be concerned about here, though, after a great trade for Julio Jones. Ruda’s immense depth at running back allowed him to give away Chubb without hesitation, and while John Brown was doing well it’s fair to expect Julio to overtake him with him. Unlike Girish, Ruda has plenty of time sitting at 5-2. The main worry for him is Brian; both these teams are looking more and more playoff bound and the focus has turned to seeding. Who will be first?</p>
