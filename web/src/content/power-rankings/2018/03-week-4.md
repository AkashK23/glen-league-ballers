<p>The storm came. Remember how I explained that our league was scoring too much and had to slow down? Well, in Week 1 our teams averaged 102 points, and in Week 2, 97.54. Week 3? Just 87.9. The scoring explosion came to a sudden stop, and taught us a lot about which teams were just getting lucky and which are the real deal. Now it’s only Week 3; we have 10 left until the playoffs even start. But we can start reaching a few conclusions.<br/><br/>10: Ashwin (-1)<br/>Someone had to lose this one, I guess. Yes, Ashwin was disadvantaged because of injuries to his running backs, but we can’t let that distract us from the s u c c that is the rest of this team. Antonio Brown is only averaging 11 points per game due to surprisingly high involvement of players like JuJu and Conner. Keenan Allen is averaging less than 9. Trey Burton and Allen Robinson s u c c too; the Bears are a rapidly improving team, but their offense is not worth investing in fantasy-wise. And do you really believe that adding back an aging McCoy and a committee-based Ajayi will make that much difference? The only positives I see for Ashwin are the upside of Wentz (as he shakes off the rust) and the consistency of Landry at flex. And yes, Landry should continue to be his flex.<br/><br/>Ashwin is 0-3 and is averaging a paltry 73.4 points a week. That’s on pace for just 954 on the season; the current record low is 971... also by Ashwin. He’s also the only one who’s gotten last place twice. If he does it again, there are some dire consequences this time.<br/><br/>9: Sid (+1)<br/>Not bad, kid. For a team that looked absolutely lost a week ago, scoring 89 is really all you could ask. There are still many, many problems with this team. Sid’s over-reliance on Cam Newton is reaching concerning levels; this week Superman scored 33% of his points (last week: 51%!).<br/><br/></p>
<img alt="" loading="lazy" src="/power-rankings/2018/03-week-4-ce96dae1.jpg"/>
<p>Cam is on bye now and backup QB Jimmy G is done (F). Sid actually has a strangely high amount of SF players, who will all suffer now. Sid’s wide receivers are unbelievably pathetic and honestly I think he needs to see a counselor about his relationship with Amari Cooper. What makes this team a little bet better than hopeless is, well, hope. Ingram and Jeffery are ALMOST back and I can’t emphasize how huge of a difference those two can make, especially Ingram as Bernard is only a temporary starter. And now we’re starting to hear trade rumors about Bell. If he does play anytime soon, Sid could be a sneaky late bloomer. For now, at least, he’s notched a win and will be looking for an upset in a very unfavorable matchup with Ruda.<br/><br/>8: Akash (-1)<br/>They are who we thought they were. Outside of one random explosion against Adit, Akash has scored in the 70’s twice and there isn’t much reason to expect drastic improvement. Luck is struggling and even on a day where Hunt can snag two touchdowns, the unbelievable skill of Mahomes still eats away at his yards and points. Coleman will be relegated to a FLEX play once Freeman returns, and apparently Phillip Lindsay likes to punch people. Now, the wide receivers here are actually pretty solid and if Akash wants to see improvement it’s likely going to come from here. Diggs (and Minnesota in general) had an off-week, but can generally be expected to play well. Enunwa has earned the trust of rookie QB Sam Darnold and should continue to get lots of looks. And Will Fuller will probably keep tearing it up on the bench while Akash forgets to start him. Meanwhile, Josh Gordon diligently studies the New England playbook in a Boston apartment, playing loud music to distract himself from withdrawal symptoms. Things could be worse, I guess. But they’re pretty bad.<br/><br/>7: Girish (+1)<br/>Meh. That’s pretty much all you can say about Girish’s performance and team. This was always going to be a high-wager bet on his wide receivers to dominate week in and week out, and they just haven’t so far. Much like Ashwin’s WRs, they are averaging around 10-11 points per week. That means other players need to step up; Watson, Lynch, and Ivory kindddd of have, but clearly not enough. It’s worth keeping an eye on this team, because if Watson can continue to score mid 20’s like this week, and if Lynch and Ivory can at least throw some touchdowns in, we have to expect improvement in the WRs and could see some big days from Girish. That certainly hasn’t happened so far, as he is one of three teams to not have a 100-point week (take a wild guess who the other two are). Meh.<br/><br/>6: Anish (-)<br/>Only two teams have scored 100+ in all three weeks this season: Will and Anish. It’s about time Mr. Binkie notched its first win, but going against opponents who score 117 and 120 is hard. Honestly, this team is really good. Record is the only thing keeping it from being ranked higher. Big Ben is performing as always at QB, and Todd Gurley is just a monster. He seems to be pretty much guaranteed 20+ points a week. Alex Collins and Davante Adams have played at the level Anish hoped when he drafted them, and though Goodwin will probably suffer greatly from the loss of Jimmy G, Anish’s capable bench receivers should pick up the slack. Strangely, one of the things holding this team back has been TE Rob Gronkowski, who has only scored 6.6 in the last two weeks combined. It has been a rough patch that the Patriots will likely claw out of soon, so expect Gronk to be the best TE soon. Anish also could work on his lineup decisions; pretty much every week he benches someone, especially receivers, who performs well. Check out those six touchdowns on his bench though.<br/><br/>5: Brian (-)</p>
<img alt="" loading="lazy" src="/power-rankings/2018/03-week-4-231bfb79.jpg"/>
<p>It wasn’t a flop by any means, but Brian had a bit of a fall back to reality this week. A close loss to a good team isn’t the worst thing ever, but right now Brian is just shy of being in that upper echelon of teams (ranks 1-4). He’s pretty much right around Anish’s level. Being 6th in PF is meh, although he’s averaging 100 a week. The crazy thing is he’s on pace for just 1008 PA on the whole season; compare to the current record low of 1058. You can’t make too much fun of him for that considering his all-time PA is by far the most of anyone (averaging 1253 per season).<br/><br/>Fitzmagic delivered pretty well (although couldn’t push Brian to the win), and Melvin Gordon continues to be one of the best fantasy backs in the entire league. Once Mixon comes back (soon), this will be a scary RB duo. Rudolph is doing pretty alright at TE, and look at Brian’s bench last week; there’s a lot of potential there. His front office announced the decision this week to begin starting some of those players, which adds some uncertainty and excitement. That might be needed if first round pick OBJ continues to struggle.<br/><br/>4: Arjun (-1)<br/>That was close. Arjun barely squeezed out a win in a Monday night thriller that came down to the final drive. We are a little concerned because his PF has dropped every week so far after a monster Week 1 (which is still the season high). The good news is Kamara and JuJu are absolutely powering this team forward; they combine for 30 or more basically every week. Matt Ryan now present an intriguing option at QB. He won’t get 40 every week, but injuries and poor play in the Atlanta secondary is forcing them into shootouts that Ryan’s fantasy owners benefit from. Arjun also owns two Rams receivers who are benefiting greatly from the development of Goff. Add in two Chiefs players and this team is going to see its players in shootouts a lot.<br/><br/>Arjun is 3rd in PF (in fact, the top four scorers are in the East) and having a great season so far. He’s part of the top class I talked about earlier, almost interchangeable with anyone above him. What keeps him just under is the constant decrease in scoring and a little less upside than other teams thanks to meh seasons from guys like Dalvin Cook and Gostkowski.<br/><br/>3: Adit (+1)<br/>The theme for Adit this season has been “overcoming adversity”. Even disregarding the holdout and subsequent trade of Bell, injuries have absolutely wrecked this squad. Second round pick Devonta Freeman hasn’t seen action since the third quarter of Week 1. Adit overcame it with a smart pickup of Matt Breida, who subsequently got injured himself last week. That was right around the same time that AJ Green got hurt and left the game for good. Both those players missed half of their respective games (throw in a minor injury to Graham too), but Adit still led the league in scoring with 112 points. All against the consensus #1 team in the league. Say what you will about the curse, but that’s pretty impressive. What this tells us is that while current injuries (Freeman still not looking good) keep this team out of the top two, the potential it has if and when they stop hindering it is pretty insane. Stairway to Evans has the most upside of any team. The only real area of concern is tight end, as Graham is struggling to gel in the Green Bay offense. Also, Adit had two elite quarterbacks and can’t seem to start the right one. Good luck choosing each week. I’d hate to be him.<br/><br/>2: Will (-1)</p>

<p>Will it ever be broken? Will has another shot in Week 12, but remains winless against Adit through six matchups. A late surge actually made for an impressive overall performance - Will has yet to score less than 100 - but it wasn’t enough. Still, we are ranking Will as the #2 team and above Adit and Arjun because the Vikings had an abysmal performance that we don’t expect to see again. If Cousins had even a normal day (and, by extension, Thielen did better as well), Will could have easily hit 115 and notched the win. Barkley continues to impress with high volume, and Michael Thomas honestly might end up being the best fantasy receiver this season. Jordan Reed is not living up to expectations at TE (notice the theme here? Tight ends are not having a good year) and Miller is an iffy flex - but guys like Adrian Peterson and Chris Carson are showing they could be a big improvement for Will at that position. Actually, I get the feeling that AP is gonna pull one of those every-other-week seasons where Will starts him in all the bad weeks. We’ll see, though.<br/><br/>1: Ruda (+1)<br/>There isn’t a very clear cut #1 right now - especially considering Ruda didn’t actually do that well this week (barely topping 80) and is only fifth in PF. The reason he is up here is the high likelihood of this week being a massive fluke, one that severely damaged his stats in this young season. Let’s be real: Tom Brady is not going to be held to 7.5 points most weeks. Or any weeks, really. Him and Hogan (although watch out for Gordon) should bounce back in incredible fashion - we’ve seen how angry the Patriots yet after losses. Let’s also consider Zeke’s meager 13.7 points per game. The Cowboys are struggling, but Zeke can’t be contained for too much longer. He’ll bump that number up to 16 or 17 pretty soon. Not much even needs to be said about McCaffrey, who looked like Bell reborn (sniff) as he obliterated a stout Bengals front on Sunday. James Conner also had a bit of an off day in a weird game against Tampa; we can expect a healthy 15+ per game from this starting stud as long as Bell keeps being Bell. And Wentz showed extraordinary trust in Ertz in his return, targeting him 10 times. Expect that usage to continue as it did last year. Really the only point of concern is the wide receivers; Ruda should probably try working some waiver magic in that department. <br/><br/><br/>For now, he’s number 1. <br/><br/><br/>But it’s close.</p>
