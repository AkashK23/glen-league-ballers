<p>In last year’s opening week, Girish and Brian faced off in their annual rivalry match. After weeks of hype, The Fantastic Gore flopped in epic fashion, managing just 59 points in an embarrassing loss. Girish was rightfully placed at the bottom of rankings and quickly became the laughingstock of the league.<br/><br/>Then he went 8-5 and won the championship.<br/><br/>The point is, we like to overreact to Week 1. Rusty NFL teams often stumble out of the gate, causing lopsided wins or odd shootouts that can’t be regularly reproduced. Basing an entire league ranking on the events of one week is ridiculous and probably shouldn’t happen.<br/><br/>But I’m going to do it anyway. Here are your opening week overreactions.<br/><br/>10: Sid (-3)<br/>Sid’s aptly-named team wasn’t actually the worst of the week, but any observations of his performance are no doubt overshadowed by the major trade struck Tuesday evening with Adit. Sending away your second-best player for two guys who can’t even play right away raises some valid questions, but there may be a method to the madness. There will likely be a point this season where Sid suddenly adds Alshon Jeffery, Mark Ingram, and LeVeon Bell to his team in rapid succession. That is nothing to laugh about. This is a power ranking heading into next week, however; anyone ahead of a certain team would theoretically beat that team if they played this coming week. And there’s little doubt that Sid would most likely lose to everyone. Cam and DJ can keep his team competitive, but until the rest of it returns we can’t expect much.<br/><br/>9: Akash (+1)<br/>I actually believe Akash’s starters will bounce back a little bit. Kareem Hunt took a backseat to a Chiefs passing game that caught the Chargers completely off guard; future defenses will plan better and force the Chiefs to run more. Fournette got hurt and didn’t finish the game. And we can expect a lot more from Ertz once Wentz returns. The problem is this team’s abysmal depth. Three players on the bench didn’t even play, and the remaining four averaged 1.6 points. This is even more alarming when you consider that Rex “Flex” Burkhead is more like Rex “Not Flex” Burkhead and needs to be replaced. If Fournette stays hurt and if Gordon continues to see low usage, Akash has simply no one to replace them with. This team isn’t good.</p>
<img alt="" loading="lazy" src="/power-rankings/2018/01-week-2-42da4373.jpg"/>
<p>8: Girish (+1)<br/>Ouch. The Nuklear Bombs blew a significant lead to their arch rival, marking the second opener loss in a row for the franchise. It was scary to see Deshaun Watson perform so terribly, especially because Hopkins was limited by extension. If performances like that continue, Girish will be way too reliant on Julio Jones to get anything done. Kinda like Matt Ryan. Olsen’s injury only makes things worse. Still, this team did score 84.5, which isn’t awful. Lynch and Hilton showed they may return to form, and Crowell inspired some hope on the bench. Again, though, it really all depends on that Texans offense. Girish took a risk investing in Houston; we’ll see if it pays off.<br/><br/>7: Ashwin (+1)<br/>Adit’s Pepper pretty much had the most mediocre day you could ask for. The strength of this team is the one-two punch at WR, and they delivered by combining for over 32 points. Ajayi bounced back from a slow start to have a monster day, though we can reasonably expect him to slow down when Wentz returns (though Ashwin has Wentz himself). Those three were pretty much the brunt of Ashwin’s points, though. Stafford won’t throw four interceptions every week, but there are valid concerns about McCoy and Trey Burton. I talked last week about this team investing in two Chicago players; the Bears offense is definitely improving, but was the investment a stretch? Both Ashwin’s defenses are meh and both his tight ends are bleh. He’s not passing 7 this week.<br/><br/>6: Brian (-1)<br/>“The top 6 are hard to rank” -Anish<br/>“Top 6 is always very close” -Anish<br/>“Like I said top 6 are close” -Anish<br/><br/>These are all texts Anish sent me during the ranking process and subsequent discussion, and in case they are hard to decipher, they mean that the top 6 teams are all good and difficult to compare. So while Brian may be in the bottom half of the rankings, his team is still good and will compete with the teams in the upper half. The Sloppy Joes pulled off a great comeback win despite starting the single worst defense of the week (and probably of all time). Melvin Gordon did exactly what he was drafted to do; score 10-20 every single week. It was great to see Sanders establish a connection with new QB Case Keenum and shine as Brian’s flex. And the ever-hyped Joe Mixon was brilliant, even managing to minimize backup Giovanni Bernard’s role. The final surge came from vaunted QB Aaron Rodgers, but not before giving Brian a major injury scare.</p>

<p>Keep an eye on that injury, though. The Packers may be forced to start legendary QB DeShone Kizer if Rodgers can’t recover fast enough.<br/><br/>5: Ruda (-3)<br/>Don’t let the three-place drop fool you; this team is dangerous and Ruda is ready to aim for a fourth consecutive playoff trip. Zeke, as expected, had only a mediocre day against Luke Kuechly and the Panther front seven, but Ruda’s pickup of James Conner was clutch as the young RB tore apart the Browns for nearly 30 points. Even though we can expect Zeke and CMC to improve, the Vikings D won’t always get 18 and Conner is a threat to fall off any week with a potential Bell return. That’s what keeps this team from staying way up high. Still, the Zeke Squad is in great shape. It will only help when Doug Baldwin returns, especially considering that Seattle may find themselves behind more often than not this season.<br/><br/>4: Arjun (-)<br/>Woah! WOAH!<br/>Pump the BRAKES!<br/>You’re telling me that the scoring leader of Week 1, with an incredible 132.3 points, is ranked FOUR?<br/><br/>Yes. Yes I am.<br/><br/>First of all, I’d just like to say that this team is really good. Kamara is a beast, Tyreek is a beast, Cook is probably a beast, JuJu is pretty much a beast, and DT is sometimes a beast. That’s good enough for me. Consider, though, that about 70 of Arjun’s points last week came from two players. Kamara and Hill are great (remember, I am a big fan of Mahomes this season and think Hill will shine), but Ingram’s impending return and a surefire shift to more running in KC will slow them down a little. At the very least, they won’t be scoring quite this much. And the rest of this team didn’t do THAT well. It’s possible Cook becomes the star he was drafted to be, and Kelce should get more than 6 yards most weeks. So really, this is a hard team to judge. Note the excellent depth on the bench, as well. For now, Arjun has to be 4, but another performance like this could rocket him up.<br/><br/>3: Anish (-2)<br/>Our highest ranked 0-1 team, Mr. Binkie lost in style late Monday night as Todd Gurley fell just short of scoring a late touchdown. Anish remains ranked high because every one of his wide receivers exploded... except for one of the two he started. Any other receiver would have given him the win. Goodwin’s injury kept him to zero points, but Anish still managed to score well over 100. The damage was fairly spread throughout the team, an encouraging sign. Gurley, Adams, and Gronk will provide an extremely strong core for this team to depend on, with Wilson, Collins, and the bench receivers capable of going off any week. Watch out for Binkie Boo this season.<br/><br/>2: Adit (+1)<br/>One word describes Adit’s performance this week: impressive. Things were not looking good after Bell ding-dong ditched the Steelers and Freeman flopped while injuring his knee on Thursday. The team’s other players rose to the challenge, however, with Brees and Evans in particular going off during a spectacular shootout in the Superdome. Brees is looking to be a great value pick while Evans has re-established his connection with Ryan Fitzpatrick (who supposedly will be replaced by Winston in a few weeks - we’ll see about that). It was also very encouraging to see Carlos Hyde be the bellcow for an improved Cleveland offense; Nick Chubb was a non-factor. Even if Brees and Evans regress, we can expect improvement from the likes of Freeman and Graham, making this team formidable for the near future.<br/><br/>Oh, yeah. There’s that little trade that happened, too. We can’t know right now whether it was a good move or not, but this is again a weekly ranking. In the short term, all Adit did was essentially add one of the most explosive receivers in the league to a roster that already hit triple digits last week. For the time being, he has to be top 3.<br/><br/>1: Will (+5)<br/>Broccoli is an edible green plant in the cabbage family whose large flowering head is eaten as a vegetable. The word broccoli comes from the Italian plural of broccolo, which means "the flowering crest of a cabbage", and is the diminutive form of brocco, meaning "small nail" or "sprout". Broccoli is often boiled or steamed but may be eaten raw. On occasion, broccoli has been known to absolutely dominate opposing fantasy teams with remarkable consistency across its roster.<br/><br/>Everyone except Will’s kicker scored 10 points. EVERYONE. Except his kicker. Cousins, Barkley, Howard, Thomas, Thielen, Reed, Miller, and the Denver D. All are great players, too. There’s really not a huge amount of concern for regression with most of these guys, because none of them (except Thomas, kinda) actually exploded. They just all played well, which will probably continue to happen. And check out his bench, feat. Adrian Peterson and Brandin Cooks. Both are capable FLEX plays. The only regression I’m expecting to see is Barkley. Before breaking out on his 68 yard touchdown run, the rookie was averaging just 2 yards per carry. We’ll need to see if he can establish more consistency. With his quads though? Will knows anything is possible.</p>
