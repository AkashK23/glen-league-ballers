<p>Well then. The storm faded away rather quickly. Remember how the league averaged 102, then 98 and I correctly predicted a drop that ended up being to 88 in Week 3? In Week 4, we averaged 110.1 points. One hundred and ten points. That is obscene. This absolute explosion in scoring made a lot of stats wonky and leaves us with more questions than answers. Ranking teams in the midst of this firestorm is difficult, but let’s try to draw some conclusions where we can. At the very least, we are far enough into the season that we can reasonably start looking at playoff odds for some teams on the outer edges.<br/><br/>10: Ashwin {0-4} (-)<br/>Scoring 97 is normally solid. You should have a decent chance of winning, probably around 55% in a normal week. This was no normal week. Brian handed Ashwin his fourth consecutive loss, officially sending this team into panic mode. Though 7-win playoff seasons have happened before, 8 is generally what you go for, and that would require Ashwin to finish the season 8-1. Yikes. Is that possible?<br/><br/>Mehhhh. Normally here I’d say something about how defensive double/triple coverages on Brown may stop happening as much thanks to the impressive performance of JuJu, but it’s clear now that there are concerns all around for a Steelers offense that was effectively shut down by the Ravens. There’s a real chance to recover next week against the Falcons - if we don’t see a vintage explosive AB day, we might have to accept that he wasn’t worth the first round pick. Keenan Allen is already a massive disappointment, and Ashwin’s running backs are basically doing nothing other than the occasional Ajayi touchdown. The most potential in this team is from its many Bears who, despite my previous criticism, put together an amazing performance this past week. Unfortunately, they are all on bye as Ashwin heads into a difficult matchup with Adit. Things are looking bleak.<br/><br/>9: Girish {1-3} (-2)<br/>Few things on Earth are worse than scoring 120 points and losing. But that’s exactly what Girish did this week, and to add insult to injury he’s dropped two spots in our rankings. That drop was only the result of Sid and Akash having unexpectedly amazing games, but still shows that Nuklear Bombs have a boatload of issues that need fixing now. Any research into previous weeks points to the idea that this week was kinda flukey and not what we should expect going forward. While it was great to see the Texans play like last year, let’s reasonably expect they fall back a little bit - maybe Watson and Hopkins combine for 40 instead of 54 in a standard week. Lynch probably won’t get 16 most weeks, and though new starter Kerryon Johnson is exciting, he has a lot left to prove in terms of consistency. TY Hilton is hurt, and Julio Jones couldn’t find the end zone if he was given a map and directions. And don’t get too excited about Yeldon - Fournette will be back eventually. Still, take one look at the scoreboard from last week and you can’t help but be a little bit hopeful that this team can recover. Girish will have to prove that with an intriguing matchup versus another up-and-coming team, our next entry.<br/><br/>8: Sid {2-2} (+1)<br/>Is Sid turning his team around just like last year - only way earlier? It kind of looks like it. Pulling off the upset of the year, Sid scored the 3rd highest PF of the season in an absolute shootout. The 253.6 combined points came just shy of third place all-time. David Johnson actually did not horribly, and while Bernard is getting replaced soon, the return of Mark Ingram should probably account for that. Alshon Jeffery, the oft-forgotten component of the early-season trade for Bell, came back swinging and could prevent Sid from going through the torture of starting Amari Cooper ever again. Sid did well without Cam(!!!!!!!), who he previously relied on a little too much. And then there’s Ridley - oh lord, Ridley - pacing the league in touchdowns. Regression is certain, but how much? I bet Ridley remains a solid WR throughout the season. No one really expects Kittle to do this again, so the TE situation is meh, and Sid pretty much doesn’t have a defense. Let’s also not forget that Sid is still a dreadful 9th in season PF despite scoring almost 130. That isn’t good. So while the hype train deservedly has gotten going, let’s not get ahead of ourselves just yet. Sid has to prove he’s the real deal by beating down Girish this week.<br/><br/>7: Anish {1-3} (-1)<br/>No more excuses for Mr. Binkie. While the 1-2 start could be explained away by a freak schedule, Anish finally got the chance to play against the second lowest scorer of the week - and responded by being the absolute lowest scorer. By 25 points. <br/><br/>Gurley is still unbelievable, and Ben should normally play a little better than he did this past week. Getting Edelman back from suspension is also a plus, but consider that he hasn’t played a down in the NFL since the Patriots Super Bowl. Against Atlanta. In February 2017. Alex Collins is meh, Gronk has been a huge disappointment, Adams is hurt, Cobb is hurt, Bernard is hu - actually, everyone is hurt. Wilson isn’t doing anything (wow, poor guy, it must be awful having a bad offensive line and bad receivers). There is some oomph to this team - it wouldn’t have scored 100+ three straight weeks to start the season - but it better show that again real soon. If Anish drops to 1-4 it’s time for desperation.<br/><br/>6: Akash {2-2} (+2)<br/>This is officially the most unpredictable team, and as a result it’s hard to rank. Last week Akash scored the second highest PF of the season, a 56 point increase over the previous week. That previous week was a 35 point decrease from week 2, which in turn was a 30 point gain from opening week. So yeah. Weird.<br/><br/>One positive is that both Goff and Luck both exploded last week, although QB controversies can be dangerous (see: Adit last year and this year). I wouldn’t get too excited about Sony Michel just yet; historically Patriots RBs almost never put together consistent good games. Coleman takes a hit from the return of Freeman, but Phillip Lindsay and Aaron Jones look like solid FLEX options. Overall there are a lot of question marks but a lot of positives as well, hence the mid-tier ranking.<br/><br/>5: Brian {3-1} (-)<br/>In one of the more shocking rankings of the week, Brian is 5. Look, it’s not that this team would normally be mid-tier. It’s just a wacky year, and we can’t justify putting Brian ahead of any of the top four right now. Still, being 3-1 is a great situation, and the Sloppy Joes have loads of talent. With Mixon returning, Brian might have the best RB duo in the league. Mixon’s explosiveness and Gordon’s consistency should earn this team a lot of wins. Aaron Rodgers is sure to pick it up soon, and Kyle Rudolph is a rare performer at a thin Tight End position. Positives stop there, though. Brian doesn’t have a single top 15 wide receiver - but he’ll be getting one in Stefan Diggs. We’ll see how that move works out. And I’ll tell Brian the same thing I told Akash: you can’t trust Patriots RBs for too long.<br/><br/>Still, this is one of the biggest games of the early season. If Brian can beat Ruda he’ll take the division lead, and there’s no better way to prove you shouldn’t be ranked 5 anymore.<br/><br/>4: Arjun {2-2} (-)<br/>This was always going to be a close game, and fans have to be disappointed to see a last-second loss. Still, Arjun is a very close second in overall PF, and we fully expect his record to get firmly above .500 and stay there. Matt Ryan and Alvin Kamara form a formidable duo, and there’s no reason to believe Hill and JuJu will combine for just 11.6 points again anytime soon. It’s going to be hard to decide which Rams receiver to start at FLEX each week, but with the way that offense is rolling Arjun can be sure both will produce at least decently. The huge factor holding this team back is Dalvin Cook and the RB2 slot. On top of being slowed by injuries, Cook is just not living up to expectations as a second round pick, and Arjun doesn’t really have depth at RB to replace him. That is the biggest thing holding this team back, and it needs to be fixed soon.<br/><br/>3: Will {3-1} (-1)<br/>Will got verrrrry lucky this week. If he had played literally anyone other than Anish, he’d be at two consecutive losses and a much scarier situation. His team is obviously very good and firmly within the top tier, but we can’t help but notice that his scoring has dropped steadily every single week of the season. Michael Thomas looked human for the first time as the Saints passing game struggled, and Jordan Howard seems to have just been forgotten in this new-life Bears offense. The good news is that Will’s Vikings are clicking, and he’ll be getting back some talented players like Peterson and Carson. There’s actually a lot of great depth on this team; just look at Tyler Boyd and Sterling Shepard’s performances the last few weeks. The name of the game for Will has been consistently good players across the board, and that looks to continue. This week he’ll need to take on an unpredictable Akash. Let’s see if he can stop the downward scoring spiral.<br/><br/>2: Adit {3-1} (+2)<br/>First in PF. Second in PA. First in standings. The comeback win capped off the third Monday night nail-biter of the season for Adit, propelling him to top-two status. He isn’t THAT far ahead of Arjun and Will - no one is - but it’s hard to rank this team any lower right now, especially considering the horrific strength of schedule it’s had to put up with. Patrick Mahomes, despite slowing down, is the #1 fantasy quarterback and Carlos Hyde has been the embodiment of consistency at RB, scoring touchdowns every week. The three-headed monster of a WR corps is what powers the Warriors, though Evans is on bye this coming week. Breida has been a decent RB2, but the real story is the return of Devonta Freeman. If the rest of this team continues to perform well, Freeman will only add to the madness. Adit (with Arjun just behind) is on pace for an absurd 1442 PF this season; compare to the current record of 1386 set way back in 2015. The only real concern right now is a lack of depth (especially as bye weeks begin) and a slow start by Jimmy Graham (especially with Eifert badly hurt). Adit looks to take down Ashwin and move to 4-1 this week.<br/><br/>1: Ruda {3-1} (-)<br/>“Few things on Earth are worse than scoring 120 points and losing”<br/>-Adit, 2018<br/><br/>One of those few things is scoring 124 points and losing. We refuse to drop Ruda any ranks after putting up such a monster performance. He was just in the wrong place at the wrong time. The Patriots, as pretty much everyone expected, rose from the dead and absolutely demolished Miami. Zeke had a monster day as the Cowboys offense actually seemed to find some semblance of identity - great news for Ruda going forward. He’ll get Doug Baldwin and Christian McCaffrey (!) back, and Kenny Golladay has been surprisingly decent as a WR2. The trade for Zach Ertz looks like a smart one, and we can reasonably expect James Conner to kick it into high gear in this inevitable shootout with Atlanta. John Brown silently watches from Ruda’s bench, waiting to strike when the moment is right. This team is absolutely firing, with the only concern being the first rumors of Bell’s return beginning to swirl. We’ll keep a watchful eye on that, but right now our attention should be on another potential shootout: Ruda vs Brian. Winner takes control of the West. We’re in for a treat with this one.</p>
