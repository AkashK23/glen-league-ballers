<h3>2015 Season Notes</h3>
<p><ul><li>The 2015 season was infamous for the amount of injuries that occurred. Most affected was Brian, who lost key players in the preseason and within the first few weeks of the regular season, such as Kelvin Benjamin and Dez Bryant. Only two of his starting players listed in the "Teams and Players" page were drafted by him.</li><li>The season began with an up-and-coming rivalry match between Girish and Brian. After weeks of banter, the game unfortunately flopped, with Girish soundly beating a heavily injured Brian's team (105 to 66), known then as Young Guns. The two teams ended up on the lower end of the standings in December.</li><li>The entire running back position fell short of expectations in 2015, dooming any run-heavy team to failure. Injuries and poor performances affected nearly every top-drafted running back, with only Adrian Peterson and Matt Forte escaping unaffected among the first round backs. Unsurprisingly, both of their owners made the playoffs.</li><li>Despite an early-season loss to division rival Ruda, Adit rocketed to the top of the standings and never fell back down, managing to win seven games in a row. Despite not having the most points scored, a favorable schedule and the increasingly dominant play of quarterback Cam Newton propelled the Men of Steel. Meanwhile, Will benefitted from many of the same things. While Aaron Rodgers did not play quite up to par, a favorable schedule and consistent performances by top players such as Adrian Peterson had Will in contention for first seed for much of the season.</li><li>Ruda and Akash dominated the league statistically. Akash had perhaps the most talented group of players in league history, while Ruda finished with an all-time record 1386 points scored at the end of the season. Ruda actually swept eventual champion Adit in two meetings as well. Only a difficult schedule and division prevented him from faring better in the playoffs.</li><li>Ruda had more points for and less points against than both championship teams, Akash and Adit. He also had the highest point differential in league history by far, at +21.4. Similarly, Sid finished in seventh place despite being a top 3 scorer in points on the season.</li><li>A major midseason trade between Brian and Sid caused a great deal of controversy. Brian gave up several players such as Jeremy Hill and Russell Wilson, leading to worries that Sid would become overpowered. Many owners vetoed the trade but it managed to pass. Sid received a boost from the trade, but it did not unfairly turn around the season, and he finished in the middle of the standings.</li><li>Late in the season, the original GIB rivalry, Arnav vs. Adit, returned. For the first time, Adit prevailed over the course of the full game, coming out on top 109-77, the largest margin of victory in the rivalry's history. Arnav made a last-minute push for the playoffs in 2015 but fell short, tying in record for the division crown but losing in points scored.</li><li>The playoffs were highlighted by Adit destroying Will once again (tying Arnav's win over Revant in 2014 for largest margin of victory in a playoff game) 225-128, and Akash narrowly edging past Ruda 216 to 197 to set up the championship game.</li><li>The 2015 championship was perhaps the wildest and most unpredictable game in the history of the league. Week 1 seemed to have sealed the deal; Akash took a 96-70 lead as Deangelo Williams shredded the Ravens and Cam Newton finally fell to the Falcons. Week 2 began with little hope for any change as the difference maintained itself throughout the afternoon games. However, Adit began steadily climbing back and soon rocketed into the lead as Akash fell apart. On the first play of the evening games, Demaryius Thomas, a disappointment all season, broke free for a 72 yard touchdown, giving Adit a lead which he never lost. Cam Newton roared back to life against Tampa Bay as Carson Palmer collapsed against Seattle, and Adit won the second week 107-48, winning the overall championship 177 to 144. </li></ul></p>
