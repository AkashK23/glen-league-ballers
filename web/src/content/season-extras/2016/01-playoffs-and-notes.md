<h3>2016 Season Notes</h3>
<p><ul><li>2016 was truly an anomaly of a year. Records were set, both good and bad, and wildly fluctuating team performances and trades defined the season. It was also the first time the decimal system was used, which was received well and is planned to continue.</li><li>16 trades were made in 2016, an absurd number considering only 2 trades had been made in the previous two years combined. Adit had the most, being involved in 7. Arnav and Ashwin were the only players to not make any. </li><li>The spread of records was also unprecedented. A record 5 separate teams achieved eight-win seasons. This was the first season with no team to reach higher than nine wins. It was also the first year that a majority of the teams (6) had winning records. </li><li>Amazingly, three separate teams broke the record for single-season PA: Will, Adit, and Sid. On the other hand, Anish and Ruda's seasons were both in the top 3 all-time for PF.</li><li>Anish began his first season in the GIB League flying high. Week 1 saw a shootout between him and playoff-bound Will (second-most combined points ever at the time), which Anish narrowly won. This started a trend as the Dream Team tore its way through shootout after shootout in the coming weeks, including a high-scoring affair with Arnav that broke Week 1's second place record. Anish's powerful combination of Drew Brees and Ezekiel Elliot proved too much for most teams to handle. After starting 4-0, a quick falter proved to be a fluke as Anish entered the final stretch at 7-2. Here, injuries to key players such as AJ Green took their toll, and Anish was unable to win the division. The Dream Team backed into the playoffs as the third seed, trying to find a much-needed savior. That help came with a man named Tyreek Hill.</li><li>Ruda came into the year after a historic 2015 season that ended in crushing disappointment. He wasted no time making amends, beating his way to a 6-1 record including a key win over Anish, a division rival. In the early season, Andrew Luck and Deangelo Williams (both actually drafted by Adit and subsequently traded to Ruda) provided a steady stream of points, and Mike Evans actually overshadowed Odell Beckham, earning the number 1 WR spot. However, Weeks 8 and 9 saw consecutive losses to Will and Anish, making the Eastern division race tighter. Ruda was up to the task and rattled off three straight wins to clinch the division, thanks in large part to the incredible dominance of Le'Veon Bell. He entered the playoffs as a strong first seed in a year when everyone else seemed jumbled together.</li><li>Will put together one of the more impressive performances in recent memory, managing to win his division despite facing an all-time record 1336.7 PA (intriguingly, one year after facing the all-time record low PA). Mad Cuz Bad, after facing a rough start to 3-3, was powered by the emergence of running backs LeSean McCoy, Demarco Murray, and Jay Ajayi (who had consecutive 200 yard performances). After overcoming adversity all season, Will entered the playoffs a second year in a row, this time looking to secure a ring. Arnav also endured a mediocre start, getting to 4-4, before finishing the season on a 4-1 roll. In fact, his entire season was defined by streaks: he had a 3-loss streak, then two separate 3-win streaks. He squeezed into the playoffs in a very tough division, but the loss of RB Melvin Gordon spelled doom for the revered franchise.</li><li>The story of Brian and Girish in 2016 was a story of pride, dismay, confusion, and a lot of "what the hell just happened". Both teams came into the season as clear underdogs after consecutive years of disappointment and poor performance. So, when both rocketed to a fast start, going 2-0 with what appeared to be a strong cast of players, there was much pride. In fact, much of the league seemed happy to finally have the former bottom-dwellers contributing. Then, things went south. For Brian, Matt Forte, Brandon Marshall, and Kelvin Benjamin just gave up or something and 2-0 quickly became 2-5. It seemed for a while that Girish would fare better, going 4-3, but he then somehow managed to rattle off 5 losses in a row. The two rivals both sat in dismay, wondering how to turn their season around. For Brian, at least, there was a chance, and through some miracle he almost made it. 2-5 became 5-5 as he scored 100+ three weeks in a row, leaving fellow owners confused. Just when Brian was on the verge of a historic turnaround, everything collapsed, however. He lost. Then lost. Then, out of the playoff race and facing a reeling Girish just for pride, managed to lose again. Both teams finished 5-8, and neither has ever managed to figure out what the hell just happened.</li><li>Adit sucked, ok? I sucked. It was a horrible season for the reigning champion and, at first, the greatest franchise in the league. Aaron's Avengers acquired Aaron Rodgers in an early trade, but he proved to be the only reliable player on a lineup of disappointments, most notably Adrian Peterson and Allen Robinson. After an 0-3 start, Adit scored 110+ twice in a row, sparking hope, but it was short-lived. The interesting story here is how Aaron's Avengers built themselves back up through an all-time record 7 trades and various acquisitions. By the late season, the team had Mark Ingram, Jordan Howard, Latavius Murray, Michael Thomas, Michael Crabtree, Travis Kelce, and Vikings D, all of whom were top 13 at their positions. It just didn't translate to wins. Adit had the second-highest PA in history, and faced 100+ point oppositions in 10 of 13 weeks. The improvement was evident, however, in the postseason. Adit actually outscored every other team except Ruda in the four weeks of the postseason, and outscored both championship teams during the championship. Ah, what could have been. Meanwhile, Sid was somehow worse than Adit, managing to lose to him in four separate weeks (?!) and finishing last in the league.</li><li>The two championship teams had 55 and 56 acquisitions on the season, compared to a league average of 29.3. The only team with more? Brian, with 72. </li><li>This season was the second time that both wild cards were out of the same division. The East proved to be the dominant division in both 2015 and 2016.</li><li>Adit narrowly avoided the shameful record of all-time lowest score in a game, topping it by 0.2 (47.2) against Ruda thanks to the introduction of the decimal system. Also against Ruda was Brian's infamous performance of 55.6 points where he chose to start Brian Hoyer, Cameron Meredith, and Don Jackson.</li><li>A notable game was Brian's upset of Anish in Week 10. Anish seemed set for another win after Zeke Elliot scored 38.9 points, putting Brian in a major hole. Brian managed to take the lead, however, when LeGarrette Blount scored 24.9 on Sunday night. The Monday night game was Giants vs. Bengals, and Brian needed Janoris Jenkins to keep AJ Green below 14 points to win. Jenkins managed to do that just barely, holding Green to 12.8 before Giants RB Rashad Jennings saved the day by running out the clock with several clutch runs. The Giants won 21-20, and Brian won 105.7-104.5 in a stunning upset. Another famous game was Anish vs. Arnav, where Anish overcame a ridiculous 300 yard day by Julio Jones to win 135.9-125.4, the second-most combined points of all time.</li><li>The first round of the playoffs was comprised of two huge victories, but the storylines were still there. The Dream Team suddenly got its act together and looked like its old self, smacking down Mad Cuz Bad with fury. Key to this was the sudden emergence of Tyreek Hill, a jack-of-all-trades for Kansas City. His consistent playmaking ability powered Anish to a major upset of Will and into the championship. On the other side, Ruda's complete dominance showed over Arnav. The league watched in awe as Le'Veon Bell laid waste to Buffalo in a snowstorm, scoring 47.8 points in the first week (pictured above). Heaven's Gates could not keep up as Bell's brilliance continued and Ty Montgomery stepped into the spotlight, and Ruda was on his way to his first championship.</li><li>The 2016 championship game was not the first good championship; in fact it was the first to not feature a comeback victory. However, it was the first to end with a somehwat close score and had intensity throughout. While Ruda was expected to win, Anish was certainly capable of pulling it off thanks to his freak playmakers such as Hill who could go off at any time. The star of the show in the first week, however, was yet again Le'Veon Bell as he routed rival Baltimore and put Ruda ahead by a solid margin. Anish still had a chance, as Bell was sat in the final week. A valiant effort proved futile as Ruda held on to his lead, mainly thanks to Arizona's defense shutting down the Rams. Anish's dream rookie season came to a close as Ruda secured his first championship, making himself the top franchise in the league. Bell was named the MVP with no dispute.</li></ul></p>
