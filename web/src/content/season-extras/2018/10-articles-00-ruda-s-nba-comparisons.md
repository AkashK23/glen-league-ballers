<p>Just to preface, these are just what I think based off my gut. They don’t have the statistics that Adit uses in his writeups, or even whatever genius formula Anish has for rankings.<br/><br/>Ruda: SA Spurs<br/>A historied franchise that is looked upon with great respect. Led by a charming HC, this team is consistently near the top. Multiple legends have come and gone from this team, as the Tony Parker/Manu Ginobili/Tim Duncan trio might be compared to OBJ/Mike Evans/Leveon Bell during the 2016 championship. Still, you would expect this team to have more postseason success, as all the high scoring records don’t mean anything if you only have one ring in recent times. The Ray Allen Game 6 shot in the finals could be compared to Dan Bailey’s missed FG in last year's championship. Also New Mexico is close enough to Texas that this fits. <br/><br/>Brian: Toronto Raptors<br/>A team that went through some dark times early, yet has found the light in recent seasons. No longer the laughingstock of the league, Brian’s team looks to be poised for a championship run. Led by a player injured for most of last season (Kawhi/Aaron Rodgers), this team’s success will depend on how their health holds up.  Consistently hyped up by fans, this team seems to engage in the most trash talk in the entire league. Still, the playoffs is a whole different entity, and as Lowry and Derozan would be happy to tell you, regular season performances don’t always carry over. Only time will tell if the Raptors/Brian’s team are just pretenders, or real contenders for the crown. <br/><br/>Girish: Cleveland Cavaliers <br/>Hopkins was basically Lebron for Girish’s team last year, as he single handedly led him to his first ever ring. The Cavs 2016 ring also was similar to Girish’s in that they overtook a heavily favored opponent. Outside of last season however? Disappointment, failure, and some more disappointment. This team also shares a weird dynamic with Brian’s team/Raptors, as while Girish has the ring, one could definitely argue that Brian has been the better team overall throughout history. Like the Cavs this year, Girish seems destined for the cellar.<br/><br/>Sid: Charlotte Hornets<br/>Even beside Sid’s threat to abandon the GIB chat, this comparison actually makes a lot of sense. Kemba can be likened to Cam this year, as the team will basically go however far either star carries them. Much like the Hornets and their never ending quest for the 8th seed, Sid seems destined for mediocrity. Flashes of brilliance (scoring 129!) are followed up by horrific performances. Also, out of all of us owners, Sid is probably the closest to Michael Jordan, so this is a good fit. <br/><br/>Ashwin: LA Clippers<br/>What happened? After a really dominant start in GIB, Ashwin seems to have stumbled. The “winner” of two Hall of Shame trophies, this team looks destined to eat the pepper this year. The closest comparison I can think for this team is the LA Clippers, specifically when they had Chris Paul/JJ Redick/Blake Griffin/Deandre Jordan. Although they never won a title, they were always fun to watch. That team, along with a winning season from Ashwin, seems like a distant memory. Here’s to hoping there’s better times ahead.<br/><br/>Anish: Houston Rockets<br/>The Rockets, while alright a couple years ago, have really bust onto the scene recently. Much like Anish’s white-hot entrance to GIB, the Rockets catapulted to the top of the NBA after adding Chris Paul. After falling just a bit short of their final goal, they look primed for another run at the title. Like Anish, they also make perplexing moves sometimes (why add a super-old and sucky Carmelo Anthony while letting Ariza leave?). Much like the Rockets are led by scoring leader and MVP James Harden, Anish is powered by scoring leader and possible MVP Todd Gurley.<br/><br/>Will: OKC Thunder <br/>Scarred by bad luck (KD leaving, Melo sucking, Roberson shredding his knee, Westbrook having knee surgery this past summer), OKC’s accomplishments have sort of been overshadowed. The same goes for Will, as despite a brutal 2016 in which he had the most PA ever, he has bounced back nicely. Always in the playoff race, Will has put together a solid if not good team every year. This might be his year for a ring, especially if Thielen continues to put together a Westbrook MVP-esque season. <br/><br/>Adit: LA Lakers<br/>First, the amount of memes that come out of Adit’s texts/teams can only be compared to the media circus that is LA. Second, like Brian often says, Adit has a slight tendency to look in the past, like Laker fans do with the Kobe Era. Still, this is a historic franchise that has a ring and has been competitive in multiple seasons. Even in seasons when this team sucks, like Adit’s 2016 season, they remain in the spotlight, evidenced by Adit outscoring all teams during the fantasy playoffs that year. This team also has a lot of iconic moments, such as the long DT touchdown in 2015, AP shredding his knee in 2016, his collapse from 5-1 in 2017, and his first round pick Leveon Bell prioritizing jet skiing over football. Also, Adit has Amari Cooper now, who I guess will commit as many dumb mistakes as Javale or Lance. <br/><br/>Akash: Boston Celtics<br/>Somewhere in between a championship team and mediocrity, Akash symbolizes the Celtics in many ways. First off, Akash’s wild statements such as “stellar at ever position” are only comparable to Kyrie believing the world is flat. Secondly, Akash’s 2017 season was destroyed by injuries like the Celtics, as the second Rodgers/David Johnson and Irving/Hayward went down, titles hopes were dashed. The team still put up a respectable effort in the playoffs, as they took the Cavs/Girish down to the wire. This year, Akash sports a deep squad capable of winning it all if it all clicks together. <br/><br/>Arjun: Utah Jazz<br/>Bursting onto the scene last year, Arjun’s journey can only be described as a wild ride. He quickly jumped out to a lead in his division, but low scoring outputs proved to be his downfall, much like the Jazz. Guided by a young, unexpected, and maybe somewhat overrated star, the Jazz and Arjun hope to reach the finals on the backs of Donovan Mitchell and Alvin Kamara. Regardless, the amount of success he has had in two years since joining the league is commendable, and it’s possible he sneaks out of a close East division. </p>
