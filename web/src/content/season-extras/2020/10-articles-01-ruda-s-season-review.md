<p>Hyatt Hotel Award: William!<br/>In 1981, the 2nd and 4th floors in the Hyatt Regency Hotel in Kansas City collapsed, leading to hundreds of deaths and huge losses. The American Society of Civil Engineers called it “one of the most devastating structural failures in U.S. history.” Similarly, William had a record collapse, with his team getting injured faster than humanly possible. It seems like we go through this every year with this team. <br/><br/></p>

<p>The Brian Trophy: Brian!<br/>This award goes to the shittiest thing in the league each year. It’s named after our worst owner, and this year, he was also the winner! It was the only thing he won all year. The single biggest stain on his year has to be his trade with Sid. Although Mixon ended up being “hurt” all year, his biggest game came after Brian gave him up, and Tyreek Hill went on to become the #1 WR in the league. He’s truly become that crazy old man on the street who babbles and no one pays him attention as he yells “NEXT YEAR IS MY YEAR”. <br/><br/><em>Honorable Mention: IBM Trade Watson<br/></em><br/></p>

<p>Bustiest Bust Award: Michael Thomas!<br/>Look at the draft picks following MT. Just look. Stud RBs, Talented WRs, and Travis freaking Kelce. MT didn’t catch a TD all year, and was a headcase on top of that. Truly the worst pick of the first two rounds. <p>Swiper No Swiping Award: Girish!<br/>This award goes to the owner who was able to score the most steals. In my mind, the middle rounds of a draft are the hardest to nail, and Girish absolutely smoked them this year. His selections of Melvin Gordon, Calvin Ridley, Kyler Murray, Cooper Kupp, and Tyler Boyd were concrete reasons for his success and playoff run this year. He took a risk in reaching for Kyler, knowing that the rest of the league wanted him, and it paid off gorgeously. <br/><br/></p>

<p><strong>Playoff Thoughts:<br/></strong><br/>-Arjun kicked things off in the 4 vs 5 matchup, trouncing Akash on the backs of his same-team RB combo of Kareem Hunt and Nick Chubb. The combination of them proved too much for Akash to overcome, especially as Jalen Hurts laid waste to his Saints D and James Conner disappeared. Arjun moved on 112.54 to 86.7.<br/><br/>-The semis were a different story for Arjun, where Hunt took a backseat to a suddenly lead-back Tony Pollard. Ultimately, the combination of Patrick Mahomes, Dalvin Cook, Derrick Henry, and David Montgomery were too much for him to overcome, as Ruda got nearly 100 off them alone and moved on with a 120.46 to 83.66 victory.<br/><br/>-In the other bracket, Girish was not only facing Sid, but the rest of the league as they were supporting the latter. Regardless, in one of the most explosive playoff performances ever, Girish put up 133 by the help of Kyler Murray and Calvin Ridley. He had relied on his stars to carry him all year, and they delivered here. <br/><br/>-Lastly, the finals, or as we liked to call it, the SONY matchup. Girish’s hopes were high early on, but Kyler’s stinker on Saturday put a damper on his spirits. David Montgomery continued going nuclear on the tanking Jaguars, putting up a cool 18.1 points. Girish could only watch in horror as the Texans D collapsed to a record -9 points. Ruda eventually ended Girish’s excellent season, and Stafford’s Lasagna was able to capture the elusive second ring. <br/><br/><br/><strong>Other Thoughts:<br/></strong><br/>-Sid climbed out of the basement and joined the #Elite, even if he wasn’t able to make a run for the money. He showed great growth in drafting and making smart trades. With the full support of the league, my bet is that he finally snags a title in the next few years.<br/><br/>-Akash continued his trend of stellar regular seasons and not-so-stellar playoffs. At some point, he has to break through, right? This feels like a Peyton Manning situation. I applaud him for having the guts to trade for 2 First Round picks, even if one of them didn’t live up to expectations. (CEH vs Drake was a fascinating debate all year though).<br/><br/>-Adit, what is there even left to say? Another solid draft and year marred by illogical player collapses. The only positive for you this year is that you won’t be subject to a Russel Wilson jersey. <br/><br/>-Ashwin has the perfect personality to play fantasy.<br/><br/>This season added the element of covid-related absences to fantasy. I think it also taught us the value of having explosive WR’s (always want a Tyreek/Devante type over some reception compiler), a good if not marginal TE, and the importance of “getting your guy”. That’s one thing I hope people take away from this year. If you feel strongly about someone, go get him. Girish reached heavy for Kyler, and Ruda moved hell and high water to get Dalvin Cook, and it worked out. Sometimes the only way is through.<br/> <br/></p>
