<h3>First Era Recap</h3>
<p>By Adit Acharya<br/> <br/>On September 4th of 2014, our group gathered at Roland Glen Road to watch the NFL season begin, kicking off our newly formed fantasy football league. For some of us, it was the first foray into fantasy, while for others it was just a new direction after years of experience. All of us, however, felt the same nervous anticipation as the reigning champion Seahawks welcomed Aaron Rodgers and Green Bay into their dark and roaring stadium.<br/><br/>Two hours later, as the Legion of Boom and Marshawn Lynch pummeled the poor Packers into the dirt, I found myself regretting my decision to start fantasy football, much less my decision to draft both Aaron Rodgers and Eddie Lacy. “Is this going to ruin the fun of football?” I asked myself. I sure wasn’t asking myself that as I soared into the championship game after a shocking playoff upset in December. The truth is, I don’t think anyone knows whether fantasy has increased or decreased our enjoyment of the sport of football. But there’s no question that we all feel the energy, and the burning desire to compete, every year once August rolls around. It is that energy that will keep our league going for years to come, and it is that energy I will try to capture in this recap of the incredible First Era of Roland Glen Fantasy.<br/> <br/><u>Incredible Stats</u><br/>Five seasons. Five champions.<br/><br/>Twelve total teams. Nine of them with playoff appearances.<br/><br/>The ridiculous ups and downs, for every team, over the past five years is best captured in the statistics we have recorded with care each year. Our records ranged from 3-10 to 11-2, though while the latter was achieved only once, the former has happened SEVEN times. Our points scored has been as low as 971 and as high as 1550, and we’ve had winning streaks of 8 games countered by losing streaks of 9 games (looking at you, Girish). There have been games where team scores dipped into the 30’s, but also games where they soared past 160. Our all-time wins range from 24 to 42, and our champions have been everything from 10-3 powerhouses to 6-7 wild cards.<br/><br/>It’s easy to suggest that our league, or perhaps fantasy in general, has become more high-scoring with time. But take a look at the scoring in 2017, which was just about as low as the first two years of the league. Hell, the highest scorer barely crossed the 1300 mark, which had been done easily in all other years. Maybe we are shifting to more points in general, but there isn’t a clear trend, and there’s no telling what will happen next year.  <br/><br/>What does strike me as a trend is the higher numbers of contenders each year. Yes, Ruda had his magical 11-2 season in 2018, but if you compare the first two years with the last three, you’ll see a far greater number of teams around the 7-6 and 8-5 mark, and I think that’s going to continue. We have learned a lot over time, and fantasy is less of a random guessing game and more of a calculated competition. Except for Sid “LUCK” Moorthy.<br/>Here’s a few more crazy facts I found:<ul><li>Girish could have an entire extra season, where he scores his average PF, and STILL not be caught up to Ruda</li><li>Every active player (except Akash, but it’s close) could have an entire extra season where they net their average amount of wins, and STILL not be caught up to Ruda</li><li>Anish is 19-20 and has scored 3885.8 points versus 3884.8 opponent points</li><li>There has been just about zero correlation between draft pick and success (in terms of wins)</li><li>Akash has quietly made the playoffs more often than he hasn’t</li><li>Ashwin held the record for best regular season for four years, and during those four years had the worst regular season of anyone three times</li><li>In fact, 66% of Ashwin’s career wins came in just two seasons</li><li>A division winner won the championship in each of the first four years. <h3>POWER RANKINGS</h3>
<p>I want to make this pretty thorough, so each franchise will get quite a bit of writing. In addition to some basic stats and a blurb, I’m throwing in a “Face of the Franchise” and an NFL team equivalent. I think I’ve made these fairly, but will be ready for the inevitable pitchforks. Also, I want to note that these rankings will include all 12 franchises that have ever been in the league. Younger or dead teams will receive a slight disadvantage, but not enough to significantly take away from their performance during their time here. Also, when I give a ranking of record and points, that’s based on yearly averages, so we can include all 12 teams. With that, let’s jump right in.<br/></p>
<p>* * *</p>
<p><u>12: SID</u><br/>Record: .406 (11th)<br/>Points PY: 1185.8 (8th)<br/>Playoff Appearances: 0<br/>Championships: 0<br/> <br/>Face of the Franchise: Amari Cooper<br/>NFL Equivalent: Cleveland Browns<br/> <br/>The main thing you’ll notice about Sid’s franchise is simply a lack of accomplishment. Sid isn’t just the only franchise to have never made the playoffs; he’s the only franchise to have <em>never had a winning season</em>. Granted, he has had three 6-7 finishes, but everyone else on this list has had at least some memorable and triumphant moments. While Sid has managed to piece together solid teams on occasion, such as in 2017, they simply never do anything of note. This past year was the closest Sid’s team has ever come to making the playoffs, and he still just didn’t feel like a playoff contender.<br/><br/>Hark back to what we do remember of Sid’s forgettable career. Perhaps what everyone associates the most with this franchise is its love-hate relationship with Amari Cooper. Mainly hate. The redrafting of him in 2018 sparked many questions, none of which were ever answered. The other thing people know? Aggressive Sid, who comes out of the woodworks every time he wins a game or two to unleash his wrath upon the unsuspecting peasants that we are. Whatever happened to him?<br/><br/>I’ve selected the Cleveland Browns as his NFL equivalent, not solely as an insult. Yes, the Browns and Sid have clearly been the worst teams of their respective leagues for some time. But the Browns also command respect as a loveable loser, and many other GMs in our league find themselves silently rooting for Sid in his quest to have a winning season. Perhaps, like the Browns, Sid has some high hopes for this coming year. Only time will tell.</p>

<p><u>11: REVANT</u><br/>Record: .577 (2nd*)<br/>Points PY: 1159.0 (10th*)<br/>Playoff Appearances: 1<br/>Championships: 0<br/> <br/>Face of the Franchise: Peyton Manning<br/>NFL Equivalent: Pottsville Maroons<br/> <br/>The fact that Revant’s face of the franchise is a player who has been retired for the majority of our league’s lifespan says all you need to know. Revant left the league after just two years, and I wouldn’t blame many in the league for hardly remembering his time here. Many of you may have actually forgotten that this franchise was moderately successful during its short life; a great 9-4 playoff season to start was followed by a “meh” 6-7 season. Not the worst, really. That’s what places Revant, weirdly, at second all-time in win percentage, although the tiny sample size obviously takes away most of the impact for that. This success led me to place the franchise slightly above Sid, but its fade from relevance and even memory prevents me from giving it any more credit for its accomplishments.<br/><br/>The Pottsville Maroons were an NFL team active from 1920-1929. You don’t know them, and no one knows them, but they weren’t bad while they were around. That about sums it up, right?</p>

<p><u>10: ASHWIN</u><br/>Record: .445 (9th)<br/>Points PY: 1162.2 (9th)<br/>Playoff Appearances: 1<br/>Championships: 0<br/> <br/>Face of the Franchise: Andrew Luck<br/>NFL Equivalent: Detroit Lions<br/> <br/>In 2014, Ashwin took our young league by storm and rocketed to 10.5 wins, a record he would hold for four years. His season ended in tragedy with a playoff upset, but hopes were high for a return to the playoffs. That return never came. In fact, Ashwin managed to have the worst regular season record <em>three times</em> in the next four years. That is truly horrific, and shows that Year 1 was quite the fluke. It’s hard to pinpoint exactly what this franchise’s problem has been, but it always seems clear immediately after draft day that something has gone wrong. One thing to note, though, is that Ashwin did randomly pull off an 8-5 season in 2016. He didn’t make the playoffs in a competitive year that saw two other 8-5 teams miss it as well, but give him some credit for putting together multiple winning seasons. That’s more than the two franchises below him can say, and even more than one of the franchise left to be named can say (you know who you are). Still, I don’t think anyone would argue that Ashwin deserves a spot higher than 10.<br/><br/>It was difficult to pick a franchise face this time. Ashwin has tended to really diversify his teams across years. I just decided to pick the most impactful player in that memorable 2014 season, which was #2-ranked quarterback Andrew Luck.<br/><br/>Ashwin, like the Detroit Lions, has finished at the bottom of the league over and over again. Though, in the distant past, we remember some exciting seasons. Barry Sanders and Calvin Johnson carried even the dismal Lions to some success, but could never get past that playoff barrier. The same applies to Ashwin.<br/><br/>Like Ashwin’s successful years, this meme is old and not relevant anymore.<br/></p>

<p><u>9: GIRISH </u><br/>Record: .375 (12th)<br/>Points PY: 1115.5 (12th)<br/>Playoff Appearances: 1<br/>Championships: 1<br/> <br/>Face of the Franchise: DeAndre Hopkins<br/>NFL Equivalent: Tampa Bay Buccaneers<br/> <br/>Girish is last in wins, and it’s not close. He’s last in points scored, and it’s not close. He’s even second to last in points <em>against</em>; imagine what would happen if he actually had even an average schedule. Everything about this franchise has been absolutely, completely, and totally abysmal. Well, <em>almost</em> everything.<br/><br/>Not many can explain what happened on that dark day in late 2017. It’s common to blame Dan Bailey, and that’s a fair opinion. But Fantasy is a game of results, and Girish came out on top. Say what you will, about either me or him, but the man won a championship and that’s something the majority of the league cannot claim. He managed to put it together for one year, and I remember it wasn’t just dumb luck. He made good trades after drafting well, and started the right players at the right time. Give him credit.<br/><br/>Outside of that one year, Girish has averaged four wins per year. Yes, four. Despite facing easy schedules every single year, this franchise has failed to stay even remotely competitive save for one run. The team is often best described as a joke and most years, Sid would probably be able to beat it. Give Girish credit for that, too.<br/><br/>All in all, it comes down to how much you value rings. Those of us who have one probably rank Girish a little higher, while those of us who don’t would probably have him last. There is no question that if Girish doesn’t win that championship, even if he still made it to the championship that year, he’d be dead last behind Sid. The stats are just too awful. But I think a boost to 9th, still a bottom-dweller, is fair.<br/><br/>I don’t think much needs to be said about the face of the franchise or NFL equivalent. Both were incredibly obvious. <br/></p>

<p><u>8: BRIAN</u><br/>Record: .430 (10th)<br/>Points PY: 1153.4 (11th)<br/>Playoff Appearances: 2<br/>Championships: 0<br/> <br/>Face of the Franchise: Dez Bryant<br/>NFL Equivalent: Los Angeles Rams<br/> <br/>Hi Brian. I know you skipped everything else and scrolled here, and after seeing the big “8” next to your name, let loose a scream of rage and almost switched back to iMessage to start yelling at me. Or, maybe, your delight at being ahead of Girish has sent you into a heavenly bliss. But at least take a few deep breaths and let me make my case, because I think this is the right spot for you.<br/><br/>Let’s start with the positives, the reasons you’re closer to the middle than the end. And yes, keep that in mind. I haven’t ranked you among the bottom-dwellers, which I would define as 9-12. You’re in the middle of the pack, in the 5-8 range. Just on the back end of it. And hey, you’re ahead of Girish! The main reason, of course, is your recent rise to prominence. Two straight playoff appearances to end the First Era is a great finish, and 15 wins over those past two years is pretty solid. You have shown a great deal of improvement in drafting, and have kept your clear knack for waiver pickups – perhaps the biggest strength of your franchise. There’s no reason to expect a significant decline because you clearly are continuing to put in as much preparation as possible.<br/><br/>But these rankings are for all five years. And just as they can (and do) hurt me for my recent performance, they must hurt you far greater for your abysmal performance during the majority of the First Era. You only had 12.5 wins after <em>three years</em>. In fact, you still had less careers wins than Revant a year <em>after</em> he left the league. Your win and point statistics are among the lowest, as well. Early on, you had a really ridiculous PA situation going on, but recently your schedules have lightened up so it’s hard to feel terribly bad for you.<br/><br/>With all this in mind, I think 8 is a fair ranking. You have clearly separated yourself from the likes of Sid and Ashwin in that you are a respectable franchise and no one is going to be happy to see you on their schedule. However, I think you need more time to even out your bad start before you can move to be considered a firmly “solid” franchise. Maybe in the ten-year rankings, who knows.<br/><br/>Your literal actual fetish for Dez Bryant earned him the title of franchise face, and I think the Rams are a good comparison, ignoring their Kurt Warner-era SB win of course. The Rams were pretty horrific for most of the last several years, but rose quickly to relevance when Sean McVay took over. However, a dream season and championship run ended in tragedy when they put up their worst performance of the year at the worst possible time. Sounds familiar.<br/></p>

<p><u>7: ANISH</u><br/>Record: .487 (8th*)<br/>Points PY: 1295.3 (2nd*)<br/>Playoff Appearances: 1<br/>Championships: 0<br/> <br/>Face of the Franchise: Ezekiel Elliott<br/>NFL Equivalent: Los Angeles Chargers<br/> <br/>Alas, Anish’s worst fears have come true and he has been ranked in the bottom half of the league. But really, as I said in Brian’s blurb, think of it as three tiers, not two. Anish is firmly in the middle tier. Who can forget his explosion onto the scene in 2016? His battle for the division with Ruda brought us some of the most exciting fantasy moments ever, and their meeting in the championship capped off a great season for an exciting young team, even if it couldn’t bring home the crown. Anish led the league in points that year and was one of five teams to finish 8-5.<br/><br/>Things went a little south from there. Not to the point of being bad, really, but he’s had 11 wins in the two years since. Two losing records, although 2018 saw some playoff contention for a time. This ended up causing his stats to become <em>remarkably</em> average. 19-20 all time, with only one point of difference between PF and PA. His scoring is very high, although he wasn’t in the league during the first two years, when scoring in general was lower. All in all, he’s had one above average year, one below average year, and one average year. To me, that comes out to “average”. He was always going to be either 6 or 7, right around the middle. It just so happens that he became 7th because our 6th place team has also been about the same level of average, just for longer. The slight disadvantage that comes with playing for less time shows itself here. Don’t take it too hard, Anish. The gap is close and this is a solid franchise.<br/><br/>There was no question of beaver boy Zeke for the franchise face after Anish went out of his way to draft him 2/3 years. I’m sure he’s happy to see that his favorite team, that he has been a lifelong fan of, is his NFL equivalent. The Chargers haven’t been <em>bad</em> for a very long time, they’ve just had a lot of mediocrity. Like Anish, they had a legendary season (2006) where they went 14-2 and just barely missed out on glory. Since then, their play has been solid, but they haven’t had playoff success. Both futures are looking alright, though.<br/><br/>Also Anish, give me my $8.<br/></p>

<p><u>6: WILL</u><br/>Record: .531 (3rd)<br/>Points PY: 1216.3 (7th)<br/>Playoff Appearances: 2<br/>Championships: 0<br/> <br/>Face of the Franchise: Adrian Peterson<br/>NFL Equivalent: Atlanta Falcons<br/> <br/>As I said, Will has been our league’s perennial “solid team”, for even longer than Anish. That’s what earns him the 6th spot as we crack the top half of the rankings. While his scoring leaves some to be desired, there’s no doubting the success of this franchise from its win total, especially in the face of some tough schedules. In 2016, Will endured the hardest schedule of all time, yet still made the playoffs. I think it was around then that fantasy owners began to respect the franchise. Will had one season where he dipped to a 5-8 record, but every other season has been above that. Meaning that, other than Ruda, he has lasted the longest of the 5-year club without a truly bad season. Very impressive.<br/><br/>The biggest blow to Will’s legacy comes in the postseason. Two playoff appearances are just about what you reasonably hope for over the course of five years, but I’m sure this franchise wishes it could forget the disaster that followed both times. After losing to me by a league-record 97 points in the 2015 playoffs, Will again lost by 66 to Anish the following year. All of the teams ranked above Will have been able to make an impact in the playoffs, and that is what keeps him right in the middle.<br/><br/>6, 9, 8, 5, 6. Those are this franchise’s win tallies each season. We know exactly what to expect from Will each season. He won’t shatter any records, but he’ll be a contender and not someone you want to play down the stretch. I suppose I was the only one who looked forward to matchups with him, until he #brokethecurse. Sad face.<br/><br/>This is another organization whose franchise face is hard to pick. I went with Adrian Peterson, who Will has owned twice, at vastly different times. First was in 2015, where AP became one of the few successful first round picks and led Will to his best year so far. AP returned to the team as a Flex in 2018, serving Will surprisingly well in a decent year.<br/>As for NFL team, I thought the Falcons suited this franchise the best. A consistent and respected threat that can’t help but falter when the postseason comes around. It always feels like they’re just around the corner from finally getting there.<br/></p>

<p><u>5: ARJUN</u><br/>Record: .500 (7th*)<br/>Points PY: 1214.2 (7th*)<br/>Playoff Appearances: 1<br/>Championships: 1<br/> <br/>Face of the Franchise: Alvin Kamara<br/>NFL Equivalent: Baltimore Ravens<br/> <br/>Statistically, Arjun is just about average, but there is clear and significant reason to rank him above the other teams: his shiny new ring, and recently earned membership card to the Champions Club. Arjun has had one of the most unique and hard-to-understand two years in league history; let me run through it briefly.<br/><br/>Arjun came in as a rookie in 2017 and stormed to a great 4-0 start, giving everyone flashbacks to Anish’s run the year before. The weird thing was, he didn’t even surpass 100 points in any of these wins. Whatever he scored, his opponent scored slightly lower. That magic ran out a bit down the stretch, but after a few losses his team as a whole picked it up and finished 7-6. Considering that another 7-6 team made the playoffs that year, it’s easy to say Arjun was a contender, but he had no chance in the tiebreaker. His PF was incredibly low, lowest in the league, at 1066. In fact, this was the sixth-lowest PF of any team in history (out of 50 teams). Give credit to a winning season, but I wouldn’t blame anyone for doubting Arjun’s abilities at this point.<br/><br/>Fast forward to 2018, and Arjun reversed every negative notion anyone had about him. Well, in a weird way. He scored a full <em>three hundred</em> more points than the year before, yet somehow ended up with a worse record. Schedules are whack, man. He wasn’t going to let that record bother him, though, as he became the first losing-record team to ever make the playoffs. His team hit its peak at exactly the right moment, and four weeks later he was champion.<br/><br/>How do you judge a franchise as odd as this? Well, the ring puts him a cut above most already. His 13-13 record over two years is decent when weighed against others. He also clearly has a knack for picking rising stars and making smart lineup moves. I don’t think many people are doubting his abilities as a GM heading into next year, and most probably look ahead to weeks against him with anxiety because of his team’s tendency to absolutely go off. Now, having only two years of experience comes into play here, and I couldn’t find reason to rank him higher than five. It was a close call with our next franchise, who also played for limited time, but in the end I’m putting Arjun at the very top of the middle tier. One more good season and he’s among the elite, and I think it’s only a matter of time.<br/><br/>There were two great candidates for face of this young franchise in JuJu Smith-Schuster and Alvin Kamara. Kamara, being a top pick and probably having a slightly greater impact, won me over in the end. When I think of Arjun’s team, I think of the Saints RB. As for NFL equivalent, Arjun I’m so sorry for associating you with your most hated rival. But isn’t it a perfect fit? The Ravens came out of nowhere to win a Super Bowl (as a wild card!) soon after they were created, and are generally a threatening team. I thought it worked pretty well.<br/></p>

<p><u>4: ARNAV</u><br/>Record: .667 (1st*)<br/>Points PY: 1226.3 (5th*)<br/>Playoff Appearances: 2<br/>Championships: 1<br/> <br/>Face of the Franchise: Marshawn Lynch<br/>NFL Equivalent: San Francisco 49ers<br/> <br/>Arnav was Ruda before Ruda was Ruda. I know it feels like a long time ago, but we can’t forget the sheer dominance of this franchise, my hated rival. The man had 26 wins in three years, and would’ve made the playoffs all three years if 2015 hadn’t had so many good teams. He was the league’s first champion, and held a LOT of records well past his departure. If this list had been made at the same time in 2017, Arnav would have clearly been at #1.<br/><br/>Just a bit of recap for those of you who have forgotten. In Year 1, Arnav obliterated opponents with both the #1 and #2 running backs of the year, Lynch and Bell. He crushed Revant in the playoffs and came back from a week 1 deficit to beat me for the championship. The following year, after a slow start, he went on a winning streak and ended at 8-5, tying Akash in record for the West division lead, but losing the PF tiebreaker. Unfortunately for Arnav, the East ate up both wild card spots (both 9-4 teams!) and he just barely missed out on the playoffs. He came back with a vengeance in 2016, going 8-5 yet again and making it to the postseason this time. There was no stopping Ruda’s team that year, but such a crazy three-year run deserves respect nonetheless.<br/><br/>All that hurts Arnav is his cut-short career. There’s no telling what would have happened these past two years, and we can’t just guess. Also, while he did win a championship, he never made a second championship appearance, like two of the three teams above him did. Considering all that, I couldn’t rank him above four. But I believe it was just to give him credit as one of the upper-tier franchises in our league.<br/><br/>Arnav had Marshawn Lynch twice, and the man was a driving force in his success. This was particularly true in 2014, where Lynch seemed to singlehandedly crush my hopes and dreams every time we faced. Lynch gave Arnav his ring, won our 2014 League MVP, and is absolutely the face of his franchise.<br/><br/>I didn’t feel it was respectful to choose another forgotten NFL franchise, so I instead chose one with plenty of past glory that has simply faded quite a bit in recent years: the 49ers. Arnav’s legacy is matched well by the dominance of the SF dynasty in the 80’s.</p>

<p><u>3: ADIT</u><br/>Record: .531 (3rd)<br/>Points PY: 1240.8 (4th)<br/>Playoff Appearances: 2<br/>Championships: 1<br/> <br/>Face of the Franchise: Aaron Rodgers<br/>NFL Equivalent: Dallas Cowboys</p>

<p>Yeah, yeah. Get your flaming pitchforks ready. Deny it all you want, but you know that this is an appropriate ranking for my team. In fact, there’s a solid argument for me at 2 as well, but after a lot of objective consideration I decided I’ve slipped to 3. Let me try to explain why, in the same calculated way I have been explaining other franchise rankings.<br/><br/>Everyone already knows about my incredible first two years, because I’ve said it a million times. Just to recap: I pulled off an awesome winning streak in 2014, going 6-1 in the second half of the season and making the playoffs, and even the championship. After a pretty heart-wrenching loss, I came right back the following year with a 10-win season, capturing first seed and smashing my way to a championship win. I’m one of only two teams to have made it to two championships, which is impressive when you consider we’ve only had five championships. That they were a long time ago doesn’t matter, because this ranking looks at all years equally. What matters is that my performance, clearly, slipped in the following years.<br/><br/>2016 was a disaster and I’ll be the first to admit that. I went 3-10 (somehow didn’t get last), kicking and screaming the whole way. That year alone is a major rap on my franchise as a whole, and the second it happened I lost any chance of keeping my title as the best franchise in the league.<br/><br/>Now here’s the part that gets, shall we say, misunderstood. If my following two years were actually bad years, there’s no way I’d be up here at 3. The thing is, they weren’t. I went 13-13 over these two years, the same record as Arjun. Only Brian, Akash, and Ruda did better than that. I barely missed the playoffs in 2017. In fact, I missed them by literally 8 points. Thanks Brian. 2018 was a slightly different story, but I do think, objectively, that I actually deserve some credit for managing to finish 6-7 despite having probably the most injuries that any team has sustained ever. I’m serious about that.<br/><br/>Those injuries do partially, but not fully, account for the disastrous end to last year. I think that if I had not lost the Reaper Bracket, I would have comfortably ranked myself at 2. I completely admit that me eating the reaper was an embarrassment to my franchise that deserves mockery, and it’s what just barely pushes me down to 3. It <em>was </em>kinda funny, to be honest.<br/><br/>Is this an analysis we can all agree with?<br/><br/>My theme has always been top quarterbacks; they’ve been at the heart of my franchise. While Mahomes might be my most recent love, veterans will remember my affairs with Aaron Rodgers and Cam Newton. I did win my championship with Cam, but I think most of you picture Rodgers at the helm of my franchise, and I agree. That magical quarterback is my heart and soul.<br/><br/>I’ve always thought the Cowboys represented me well, much as I hate to admit it. Their 90’s dynasty seems about the same as my early success, but they followed it with some bad years reminiscent of my 2016 debacle. However, ever since Romo and Dak came in, they’ve been inevitably “decent” almost every year, which I think describes my last two years well.<br/></p>

<p><u>2: AKASH</u><br/>Record: .563 (3rd)<br/>Points PY: 1262.8 (3rd)<br/>Playoff Appearances: 3<br/>Championships: 0<br/> <br/>Face of the Franchise: Leonard Fournette<br/>NFL Equivalent: Kansas City Chiefs<br/> <br/>It’s weird, but Akash has quietly been one of the most consistently successful franchises in the league. After a rough start in 2014, he has scored 8+ wins <em>every year since</em>. This is surprisingly not far off from Ruda. The PF tells the same story too, with Akash having overtaken me in yearly scoring in 2018. Because of a lack of impact in the postseason, most people probably don’t think of this franchise as having been elite, but it certainly is. Let’s go over Akash’s career.<br/><br/>2014 was a horrific start for our first commissioner. Akash avoided dead last only because Girish is complete trash, stumbling to a 3-10 record. Yucky. His team was named “It Is Comeback Time”, and it turns out that statement was true, just a little early. Akash had a fantastic 8-5 season in 2015, with an excellent 1348 PF. It looked like he got the short end of the stick in being matched up with red-hot scoring leader Ruda in the playoffs, but he managed to pull off the upset and headed to the championship. After taking a solid lead in the first week, his team fell apart (and yes, Gurley sat out) and he missed out on a ring. Determined to make it back, Akash stormed to another 8-5 season in 2016, yet somehow ended up as <em>sixth seed</em>. That was a weird year. It’s not even like he had a low score and got lucky, with 1261 points. Not sure what happened there. Akash went 8-5 <em>yet again</em> in 2017, this time capturing a wild card spot despite losing both David Johnson and Aaron Rodgers in honestly one of the most impressive seasons we have ever seen. Again he lost in the playoffs, this time just barely losing to eventual champion Girish. Finally, Akash pulled off his best season yet last year, going 9-4, nabbing second seed, and setting a franchise record for points while scoring the fourth most of all time. A collapse in the playoffs cost him again, however.<br/><br/>There are two things you should have noticed in that recap. One, Akash’s four consecutive winning seasons are an incredible feat. Two, this man cannot figure out how to do anything in the playoffs. Year after year he falls apart at the worst possible time, ending up with an abysmal playoff record of 1-3. Obviously, I didn’t let this hurt him too much, since he’s second, but it should make it clear why the gap is so small between my franchise and his. Akash is definitely elite, but he needs to do something with all this regular season success before he enters a bad phase.<br/><br/>Akash had Leonard Fournette in both 2017 and 2018, his two best years. I think most of us associate Fournette with this franchise, although many of us basically associate any Wild Bois™ with it. I also think the KC Chiefs are the best representation of this organization, with sustained success over the past several years but no titles to show for it. Akash, like the Chiefs, seems to have a tendency to end up in crazy games and have polarizing players as well.<br/></p>

<p><u>1: RUDA</u><br/>Record: .656 (2nd)<br/>Points PY: 1343.8 (1st)<br/>Playoff Appearances: 4<br/>Championships: 1<br/> <br/>Face of the Franchise: Odell Beckham<br/>NFL Equivalent: New England Patriots<br/> <br/>There are plenty of ridiculous statistics I could present you to convince you that Ruda is the best franchise, but no one needs any convincing. I’ll just stick with this, then: the third highest PF of all time was Anish in 2016, with 1374, and a couple franchises have never even crossed 1300. Ruda’s <em>average</em> PF every year has been 1344. That is simply unbelievable.<br/><br/>Perhaps the most shocking fact about this franchise is that it somehow only has one championship. It feels like Ruda has been the best team in the league for the past three or four years straight, and indeed a random and unlucky ending to the 2017 championship is all that has prevented Ruda from having two rings. 2018 was nothing short of <em>magnificent</em>, as Ruda somehow exceeded all expectations and scored 1550 points while setting the long-coveted win record. To put that in context, no one has ever even scored 1400 points save for this; the second-highest point total of all time is 1386. Here’s what’s ridiculous: Ruda holds that one too. Four playoff appearances in five years is about as good as you can possibly get in a rather random game like fantasy, and even his first “bad” year was just a mediocre 5-8, earning 7th seed.<br/><br/>That season was followed up by leading the league in scoring and sweeping the eventual champion, despite the early playoff exit after a great 9-4 season. Ruda put it together for a complete season next year, going 9-4 again and winning the championship over a rolling Anish. He made a second consecutive championship after an 8-5 2017 season, losing just barely; the closest championship game of all time. And of course, this was followed by the greatest regular season of all time. Ruda scored 100+ in <em>every week</em> of 2018 except for one. I don’t even know how that’s possible. There have been teams that barely top 100 a few times in a season. Ruda, meanwhile, averaged almost 120 a week. Scoring 120 is often the highlight of a season for a team, and Ruda averaged it every week. That’s just impossible.<br/><br/>I don’t even have more to say about this franchise. There is no need to make it more clear that Ruda has been the top team of the First Era, and there’s no reason to expect his excellence to drop off anytime soon. He’s the New England Patriots without a doubt, and his open love for Odell “Hotel” Beckham makes the former Giants WR an easy choice for face of the franchise.<br/></p>
